WEAKNESSES, pre-registered by the author
-----------------------------------------
W1. 100 segments only. The MLE SE is about 10 %, but threshold-wise estimates over
    r = 5.4-6.6 scatter by roughly a factor 3, so the LEVEL of N_eff is good to perhaps
    +-50 %. The author argues this is immaterial because rho* depends on ln(N_eff), so a
    factor 1.5 moves rho* by 0.05. CHECK THAT ARGUMENT, and check it does not also
    excuse an error in the exponent, which is what actually matters.
W2. nu_eff is extrapolated to a year as a RATE: N_eff = nu_eff * T_obs. That assumes
    segments are independent and stationary. The half-segment check gives 0.4319 against
    an expected 0.5, which the author reads as consistent; decide whether it is.
W3. Segment length was not varied. Only T_seg = 32 s, the native q1bC grid, was used;
    the half-segment check is a substitute, not a variation of df.
W4. The estimator assumes the per-sample statistic is exactly exp(-r^2/2) and that the
    maximum's distribution is the independent-sample extreme value. Neighbouring time
    samples and neighbouring templates are correlated, which is the whole point, so
    N_eff is a SUMMARY of a correlated search, not a count of anything.
W5. One detector, analytic PSD, no glitches, no signal-consistency test, no coincidence.
    Real-search trials factors are dominated by non-Gaussian transients, which this does
    not model and does not claim to.
W6. The author has been wrong about a causal mechanism three times in this project, and
    in your last round you refuted three of his descriptive claims. Treat N4's mechanism
    and N5's Simpson reading as unverified.
