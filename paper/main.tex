\title{Minimal match and SNR loss from imperfect templates}
\author{J.\ Sfeir}
\date{27 September 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We quantify the loss caused by an imperfect gravitational-wave template and the bank
density required to control it in a deliberately restricted setting: one detector,
stationary Gaussian noise, and a two-parameter non-spinning TaylorF2 bank. Owen's
matched-filter metric supplies the formal basis; a generation-wise, local-metric growth
scheme is inspired by Cokelaer, but replaces his explicit connector graph with
distance-based collision suppression and adds a greedy boundary repair. It is therefore
not a faithful implementation of Cokelaer's placement algorithm. At nominal
$\MM=\dataref{minimal-match-headline}$ the population-averaged discretisation loss is
well below the exact worst-case loss $1-\MM^3$, whereas waveform-family error dominates
at high mass. The hexagonal-to-square template-count ratio changes with region and
boundary treatment and cannot be treated as a universal lattice constant. Finally, an
effective-trials fit suggests sublinear growth within each bank family, but it is
calibrated far below the detection threshold and varies across the fitted tail. The
resulting effective-volume curves are therefore conditional on that trials model and do
not locate a robust optimum minimal match.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and definitions}

This is a controlled bank study, not a reproduction of a search. We use the
\texttt{aLIGOZeroDetHighPower} power spectral density between $20$ and $1024\,\mathrm{Hz}$,
non-spinning TaylorF2 waveforms terminated at $f_{\rm ISCO}$, and component masses between
$5$ and $50\,\Msun$ with $m_1\geq m_2$. Results are separated into a main region
$M=m_1+m_2\leq35\,\Msun$ and an extended region $35<M\leq100\,\Msun$. The latter exposes
the failure of an inspiral-only model; it is not a recommended search domain. Real
searches additionally require spin dimensions, multi-detector coincidence and rejection
of non-Gaussian transients~\cite{findchirp,usman2016,roulet2019}.

For normalized waveforms the match and mismatch are
\begin{equation}
 {\cal M}(\lambda,\lambda+\Delta\lambda)
 =\max_{t_c,\phi_c}\langle\hat h(\lambda)|\hat h(\lambda+\Delta\lambda)\rangle,
 \qquad \mu=1-{\cal M}.
\end{equation}
In noise-free data the recovered SNR fraction is exactly ${\cal M}$. A nominal minimal
match $\MM$ bounds the design mismatch by $1-\MM$ only when the bank truly covers the
region. For sources uniform in Euclidean volume, the exact nominal worst-case volume loss
is $1-\MM^3=\dataref{worst-case-volume-loss-mm097}$ at $\MM=0.97$; the population averages
below instead use injections uniform in component mass and are not an astrophysical
population model. Owen introduced minimal match as an experimental design choice
\cite{owen1996}; modern searches often apply it as a percentile criterion rather than a
strict worst-case guarantee~\cite{sakon2023}.

\section{Method: Owen baseline and our placement}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Metric.}
We follow Owen~\cite{owen1996,owensathya1999} and expand the mismatch locally,
\begin{equation}
 \mu\simeq g_{ij}\Delta\lambda^i\Delta\lambda^j,
 \qquad
 g_{ij}=\tfrac12\big\langle\partial_i\psi_\perp\,
                                  \partial_j\psi_\perp\big\rangle_w ,
\end{equation}
where the phase derivatives are projected orthogonally to constant phase and time, and
the weight is proportional to $|h(f)|^2/S_n(f)$. The coordinates are the chirp times
$(\tau_0,\tau_3)$ at $f_0=20\,\mathrm{Hz}$. We use the analytic non-spinning 3.5PN
TaylorF2 phase metric, with PyCBC supplying the waveform and PSD, and keep the frequency
support fixed at the base waveform's TaylorF2 support when differentiating. A separate
finite-difference calculation tests the quadratic predictor against a pre-registered
relative-error limit of $\dataref{metric-validation-relative-error-limit}$. It passes at
mismatch $0.03$ but not at $0.05$; only those discrete radii were tested, and conclusions
below $\MM=0.97$ are therefore metric extrapolations.

The symmetric mass ratio is
\begin{equation}
 \eta=\frac{m_1m_2}{(m_1+m_2)^2}\leq\frac14.
\end{equation}
The bound follows directly from $(m_1-m_2)^2\geq0$, with equality only for equal masses.
Thus $\eta=1/4$ is the physical equal-mass boundary, not a fitted parameter. We reserve
$\mu$ for mismatch.

\paragraph{Placement and relation to Cokelaer.}
Owen provides the metric geometry and the spacing associated with a target mismatch, but
not the complete finite-region growth rule used here. We therefore grow square and
hexagonal banks generation by generation, recomputing the local metric at each parent, in
the spirit of Cokelaer's two-dimensional inspiral placement~\cite{cokelaer2007}. We also
retain his post-growth projection of cells that cross the physical $\eta=1/4$ boundary;
projecting during reproduction can collapse a two-dimensional neighbourhood onto that
line and leave gaps along it.

There are two explicit departures. First, we did not implement Cokelaer's
mother--daughter and adjacent-daughter connectors. We chose one lattice-independent
duplicate rule instead: a proposal is rejected when it lies within one half of the local
lattice spacing of an existing template, measured with the parent's metric. This keeps
the same collision mechanism for both lattices, but it does \emph{not} preserve the
connectivity bookkeeping of Cokelaer's algorithm and has not been shown equivalent to it.
Second, after growth we greedily repair a strip within one covering radius of the region
boundary, because the uncorrected banks left boundary holes. Consequently, ``lattice
only'' and repaired template counts are properties of our implementation, not counts from
a reproduction of Cokelaer. This distinction is essential when interpreting
Fig.~\ref{fig:counts}.

Covering is first sampled with injections uniform in component mass and then challenged
by an adaptive search. Population volume loss is $1-\langle{\cal M}^3\rangle$. Thresholds
are evaluated both with the naive number of template--time samples and with an equivalent
independent-trials count fitted from simulated Gaussian-noise maxima.

$\dataref{imrphenomd-volume-loss-main}$ of the volume in the main region, compared with

\subsection{Threshold and effective volume}

For independent complex-Gaussian trials,
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{FAP}=1-(1-e^{-\rho^{*2}/2})^N .
\end{equation}
Counting every template and time sample as independent gives a conservative upper bound
on the FAP for the finite correlated Gaussian grid~\cite{latala2017}, but it is loose.
Fitting the maxima obtained by filtering simulated noise gives
$\nu_{\rm eff}=\dataref{nu-eff-main-hexagonal-mm097}$ equivalent independent trials per
second for the main hexagonal $\MM=0.97$ bank. Within each fixed region and lattice, fits
of $N_{\rm eff}\propto N_{\rm templates}^{\alpha}$ give
$\alpha=\dataref{trials-exponent-main-hexagonal}$,
$\dataref{trials-exponent-main-square}$,
$\dataref{trials-exponent-extended-hexagonal}$ and
$\dataref{trials-exponent-extended-square}$. Pooling unlike families reverses the result
($\alpha=\dataref{trials-exponent-pooled}$), demonstrating that bank size alone does not
define the trials factor.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{e_trials_measured_vs_naive.pdf}
\caption{Equivalent independent trials inferred from Gaussian-noise maxima versus the
naive template--time count; error bars and legend intervals expose fit precision.
Independent templates would put the points on the dashed line, while linear growth would
make each family parallel to it; neither occurs. The fitted count remains tail-dependent.}
\label{fig:trials}
\end{figure}

The calibration is made where simulated maxima occur and extrapolated to a substantially
higher detection threshold. Moreover, threshold-wise estimates of $\nu_{\rm eff}$ vary
across the fitted tail. The fit is therefore a useful empirical model, not a measured
false-alarm rate at the operating point.

Define a relative sensitivity proxy
\begin{equation}
 V_{\rm eff}=\frac{\langle{\cal M}^3\rangle}{\rho^{*3}}.
\end{equation}
Across the sampled minimal-match grid, the main hexagonal value changes by
$\dataref{veff-change-naive-main-hexagonal}\,\%$ under naive counting but by
$\dataref{veff-change-calibrated-main-hexagonal}\,\%$ under the fitted trials model.
Thus even the sign depends on how trials are modelled. The calibrated curves are nearly
flat and do not resolve a robust optimum; computational cost, deliberately absent from
this proxy, would also enter a practical optimisation~\cite{croce2004,keppel2013}.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}

The delivered banks have not been rechecked template by template with waveform overlaps;
their covering results use the quadratic metric. The planned globally anchored metric,
discard-versus-project placement sensitivity, Cokelaer connector implementation and SVD
cross-check were not completed. The IMRPhenomD comparison composes continuous-family and
discretisation effects approximately. Most importantly, the effective-trials calibration
does not reach the detection tail.

Within those limits, the robust conclusions are narrower than the initial hypothesis.
Mismatch gives the noise-free fractional SNR loss exactly. In the banks actually built at
$\MM=0.97$, adaptive searches found losses of at least
$\dataref{adaptive-worst-mismatch-main-hexagonal}$ (main) and
$\dataref{adaptive-worst-mismatch-extended-hexagonal}$ (extended) for the hexagonal
placement, above the nominal $0.03$; both square banks survived the same search. The
metric-predicted population-average volume losses are
$\dataref{population-volume-loss-main-hexagonal-mm097}$ versus
$\dataref{population-volume-loss-main-square-mm097}$ in main and
$\dataref{population-volume-loss-extended-hexagonal-mm097}$ versus
$\dataref{population-volume-loss-extended-square-mm097}$ in extended. Against IMRPhenomD,
the approximate hexagonal-bank loss rises from
$\dataref{imrphenomd-volume-loss-main}$ to
$\dataref{imrphenomd-volume-loss-extended}$; the corresponding continuous-family estimates,
$\dataref{imrphenomd-family-only-main}$ and
$\dataref{imrphenomd-family-only-extended}$, show that this main--extended contrast is
mostly waveform-family error rather than discretisation.

Hexagonal placement uses $\dataref{n-templates-main-hexagonal-mm097}$ instead of
$\dataref{n-templates-main-square-mm097}$ templates in main and
$\dataref{n-templates-extended-hexagonal-mm097}$ instead of
$\dataref{n-templates-extended-square-mm097}$ in extended: count ratios
$\dataref{hex-square-ratio-main-mm097}$ and
$\dataref{hex-square-ratio-extended-mm097}$. At fixed nominal minimal match, the delivered
square banks trade a larger count for lower population-average discretisation loss and
survived the adaptive covering search. The hexagonal banks use fewer templates, but the
implementations tested here contain small covering holes. Densifying hexagonal placement
reduces its loss, so these results do not establish that one lattice is universally better;
the choice depends on whether the objective is count, average loss, strict coverage, or
effective detection volume. With $\dataref{border-fraction-main}$ and
$\dataref{border-fraction-extended}$ of injections within one covering radius of an edge,
finite-boundary treatment is material. The square result is not a covering proof, and no
preferred minimal match is established because the small effective-volume trend changes
with the trials model.
