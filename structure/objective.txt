GOAL
====
Five questions about how imperfect a gravitational-wave template can be before a
signal is lost, and how dense a template bank has to be so that it never is.

The setting is fixed (see SETTING below): non-spinning compact binaries with
component masses in [5, 50] Msun, one detector, Gaussian stationary noise with a
known PSD, frequency-domain templates.

1. For a true signal h and a template u at offset parameters, derive the recovered
   matched-filter SNR in terms of the match between them, exactly and then to
   leading order in the mismatch. Say when the signal is lost -- recovered SNR
   below the threshold rho* -- as a condition on the optimal SNR and the match.
   Use one concrete, physical imperfection as the worked example: a TaylorF2
   template recovering an IMRPhenomD signal of the same masses, and then the best
   TaylorF2 template for it (the fitting factor). Across the mass range, where does
   that model error cost more SNR than the bank spacing does?

2. Compute the match metric g_ij in chirp-time coordinates (tau_0, tau_3)
   numerically, from matches of TaylorF2 waveforms maximised over time and phase,
   and compare it with the analytic metric of Owen (1996) / Owen & Sathyaprakash
   (1999) built from the phase derivatives and the noise moments. Across the mass
   range: how well do they agree, and at what mismatch does the quadratic
   approximation match ~ 1 - g_ij dtheta^i dtheta^j stop being good enough to
   place a bank with?

3. Derive rho* from a target false-alarm probability over the whole analysis, for
   Gaussian noise, with the matched-filter SNR maximised over phase and with a
   trials factor ~ N_templates x N_independent_time_samples. The threshold then
   depends on the bank, and the bank's size depends on the minimal match: close
   that loop. How strongly does rho* actually depend on the minimal match, and is
   the dependence large enough to matter for question 5?

4. Place banks at a target minimal match MM in (tau_0, tau_3), with square and with
   hexagonal lattices. Give N_templates as a function of MM for both. Then, by
   Monte Carlo over injected signals drawn across the mass range, measure the
   fraction whose best match in the bank falls below MM, for each lattice, with its
   Monte Carlo uncertainty. The headline point is MM = 0.97; report the curve.

5. The textbook statement is that a bank at minimal match MM loses a fraction
   dV/V ~ 3(1 - MM) of the detection volume. Measure the volume loss from your
   injections -- worst case and averaged over the population -- as a function of
   MM, across the mass range, including where the metric in (tau_0, tau_3) varies
   fastest. Where does the rule hold, where does it break, and by how much? Then
   make everything in 1-4 compatible with each other and with the published
   results in SOURCES, and say plainly where it leaves the question: how imperfect
   can a template be, and how dense must the bank be.


SETTING -- these choices are fixed, do not revisit them
======================================================
The source of truth is ~/gw-minimal-match/wiki/conventions.md, "Non-negotiable
choices", which also records the alternative rejected for each. In brief:

- Detector and noise: one detector, stationary Gaussian noise, one-sided PSD
  `pycbc.psd.aLIGOZeroDetHighPower`, analytic, sampled on the analysis frequency
  grid.
- Band: f_low = 20 Hz, f_high = 1024 Hz.
- Segment length: the next power of two above the longest TaylorF2 duration in
  range (the 5+5 Msun corner from 20 Hz); df = 1 / T_seg.
- Parameter space: m1, m2 in [5, 50] Msun, m1 >= m2, no spin.
- Bank coordinates: chirp times (tau_0, tau_3) referenced to f_0 = f_low = 20 Hz.
- Waveforms: TaylorF2 (3.5 PN phase) for the metric comparison and everything
  downstream of it; IMRPhenomD only as the "missing physics" signal in question 1.
- Inner product: <a|b> = 4 Re int a(f) b*(f) / S_n(f) df. Match = normalised inner
  product maximised over time and phase. Mismatch = 1 - match.
- MM = 0.97 is the headline point; every curve is computed as a function of MM.

WHAT YOU CANNOT GET FROM THE SOURCES
====================================
- The target false-alarm probability and the observation time behind the trials
  factor. The project has not chosen them. Carry both as free parameters, show how
  rho* moves over a range that brackets what searches use, and state which values
  the headline numbers use and why. Do not present that choice as a result.
- Whether a chirp-mass cut on top of the component-mass box changes any of the
  conclusions. Decide whether it is worth testing; if you test it, show it.
- The injection population: uniform in what, and how many draws are enough for the
  covering fractions to be resolved at the level question 4 needs. State the
  choice and the Monte Carlo error it buys.
- Where "the metric varies fastest" actually is in this range. Find it, do not
  assume it.
- Whether the five questions are the right five. If answering them turns up
  something that makes one of them the wrong question, say so.

Do not assume the framing is correct because we wrote it.
