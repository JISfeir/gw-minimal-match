GOAL
====
The next job on the minimal-match project. Job q12 answered questions 1 and 2 and was
reviewed. The review found that part of question 1 was mis-posed and that question 2
lacked an operational test. This job fixes both, then answers the bank, threshold and
volume questions in dependency order: the threshold needs the bank's size, and the volume
needs both.

Six parts, in this order. Each names the claim in ~/gw-minimal-match/structure/claims.yaml
it serves.

A. Recovered SNR: the expected value and the observed statistic   (mismatch-snr-loss)
   1. In noise-free data a unit-normalised template recovers rho_rec = rho_opt * match
      exactly, so the fractional SNR loss is exactly the mismatch mu = 1 - match. Only
      mu ~ g_ij dtheta^i dtheta^j is leading order. State this; do not re-derive it at
      length.
   2. In stationary Gaussian noise the observed statistic, maximised over phase, is a
      random variable. Derive its distribution given rho_opt and the match (check, do not
      assume, that E|z|^2 = rho_opt^2 match^2 + 2 in the normalisation you use), and from
      it the probability that a signal with given rho_opt/rho* and match falls below rho*.
   3. Validate by injection: coloured Gaussian noise from the PSD, a signal added, an
      offset template filtered against it. Compare the measured distribution of the
      statistic with the prediction at two or more mismatches.

B. What the TaylorF2 / IMRPhenomD mismatch measures   (model-error-decomposition)
   Job q12 found that 73-99.7 % of the same-mass mismatch comes from TaylorF2 ending at
   f_ISCO. Report two comparisons, and call neither of them simply "model error":
   1. Common support: IMRPhenomD truncated at the TaylorF2 f_ISCO of the same masses,
      against TaylorF2 -- at the same masses, and at the best-fit TaylorF2 (fitting
      factor). This isolates the difference between the two models below f_ISCO.
   2. Full signal: untruncated IMRPhenomD against TaylorF2 ending at its f_ISCO. This is
      the total loss from using this template family.
   For a template with no support above f_c the two are related exactly by
   match_full = sqrt(fraction of the signal's SNR^2 below f_c) * match_common. Verify the
   identity numerically. It is a check, not a result.

C. When the quadratic metric is good enough to place a bank   (metric-vs-owen)
   Job q12 verified that, with the cutoff held fixed, the numeric metric agrees with the
   analytic 3.5PN projected-phase metric built by Owen's method to within 0.74 % over 28
   points. Spot-check that at a few points and reuse it; do not recompute the grid.
   What is missing is an operational, coordinate-invariant test. For displacements whose
   predicted mismatch is fixed at mu_pred = 1 - MM, in every direction, compare mu_pred with
   the exact mismatch computed from matches, and take the worst relative error over
   direction. Do it twice:
     (i)  fixed support -- both waveforms cut at the base point's f_ISCO;
     (ii) physical templates -- each waveform ends at its own f_ISCO.
   Map the worst error over the mass range and apply the pre-registered criterion in
   SETTING. That (ii) is not quadratic is a physical consequence of the moving cutoff, not a
   failure of the analytic metric: report it as that.
   Do not report "where the metric varies fastest" through a derivative or a norm of the
   components of g_ij. Those depend on the coordinates.

D. Template banks and covering   (covering-at-mm097)
   1. Place banks at minimal match MM in (tau_0, tau_3) with square and with hexagonal
      lattices, both spaced for the same covering radius, i.e. the same MM. With Owen's
      spacing (Owen 1996, Eq. 3.16) the worst point of a square cell, its centre, has
      mismatch exactly 1 - MM, so both lattices should cover; the hexagonal one needs fewer
      templates. Give N_templates(MM) for both, and compare their ratio with the
      constant-metric expectation, hexagonal/square = 0.770.
   2. By Monte Carlo over injected TaylorF2 signals, measure the fraction whose best match
      in the bank falls below MM, per lattice, with its Monte Carlo uncertainty. With
      correct spacing that fraction is zero up to Monte Carlo error. Any excess is evidence
      of metric variation, boundary effects, discretisation or an implementation error:
      attribute it, with evidence.
   MM = 0.97 is the headline point; report curves over a range of MM.

E. Detection threshold   (detection-threshold)
   1. First, the day-2 check: on signal-free simulated Gaussian noise, <|z|^2> = 2 and the
      measured tail of the statistic matches the analytic one. No threshold is trusted
      before this passes.
   2. Derive exactly the tail for one template and one time sample: P(|z| > rho*) for the
      phase-maximised statistic in Gaussian noise.
   3. Over the whole analysis, FAP = 1 - (1 - p)^N_eff for an effective number of trials
      N_eff. N_templates x N_time_samples is an upper bound, not the answer: neighbouring
      samples and neighbouring templates are correlated. Compare the naive bound with at
      least one estimate that accounts for the correlation time of the matched-filter
      output; include template correlations if it can be done cheaply, and say so if not.
   4. Close the loop MM -> N_templates (from D) -> N_eff -> rho*. Report rho* both at fixed
      N_eff and closed-loop, over the MM range.

F. Volume   (volume-loss-rule)
   Keep these separate; never merge them into one number:
     - 1 - MM^3: the exact worst-case fractional volume loss at fixed threshold, for
       sources uniform in Euclidean volume;
     - 3(1 - MM): its linear expansion;
     - 1 - <M_bank^3>: the population-average discretisation loss, measured from D's
       injections.
   Present separately: TaylorF2 injections (pure discretisation); IMRPhenomD injections
   (model error plus discretisation); fixed threshold; closed-loop threshold from E.
   Then answer: once the threshold loop is closed, is there a minimal match beyond which a
   denser bank no longer gains detection volume? A rough estimate (rho*^2 ~ 2 ln(N_eff/FAP),
   N_templates ~ (1 - MM)^-1 in two dimensions) suggests that between MM = 0.97 and 0.99
   the threshold increase costs about as much volume as the finer bank recovers. That is a
   hypothesis to test, not a result.

Every part reports the main region and the extended region separately (see SETTING). The
main region carries the headline numbers; the extended region is where the model's
breakdown is shown.

SETTING -- these choices are fixed, do not revisit them
======================================================
Source of truth: ~/gw-minimal-match/wiki/conventions.md. In brief:

- Detector and noise: one detector, stationary Gaussian noise, one-sided PSD
  `pycbc.psd.aLIGOZeroDetHighPower`, analytic.
- Band: f_low = 20 Hz, f_high = 1024 Hz. Segment: next power of two above the longest
  TaylorF2 duration in range; df = 1 / T_seg.
- Masses: m1, m2 in [5, 50] Msun, m1 >= m2, no spin. Two regions, always reported apart:
    main      M = m1 + m2 <= 35 Msun -- the inspiral low-mass search range in FINDCHIRP
              (Allen et al. 2012, Sec. I), which covered 2 < M < 35 Msun;
    extended  35 < M <= 100 Msun -- where TaylorF2 cut at ISCO stops being usable.
- Coordinates: chirp times (tau_0, tau_3) at f_0 = f_low = 20 Hz.
- Templates: TaylorF2 (3.5 PN phase), ending at f_ISCO = 1 / (6^{3/2} pi M), the LAL
  default, recorded as a convention. IMRPhenomD to f_high is the physical signal in parts
  B and F.
- Inner product <a|b> = 4 Re int a b* / S_n df. Match: normalised inner product maximised
  over time and phase.
- Notation: mu = 1 - match is the mismatch. Do not write calligraphic M for the match or for
  the mismatch -- job q12's legend error came from exactly that -- and M is total mass.
- Analytic metric: the 3.5PN projected-phase metric built by Owen's method, not the
  historical 1PN or 2PN matrices printed in Owen 1996 or Owen & Sathyaprakash 1999.
- Quadratic-validity criterion (pre-registered): the metric is good enough to place a bank
  at MM wherever the worst relative error of the predicted mismatch, over direction, is at
  most 10 % at mu_pred = 1 - MM. At MM = 0.97 that bounds the guaranteed match between
  0.967 and 0.973.
- Lattice comparison: equal minimal match (equal covering radius), not equal density.
- MM = 0.97 is the headline; curves are functions of MM.


FIGURE RULES
============
Apply to every figure you make.

F1. Measurement against prediction. A figure whose y-values are computed from its x-values
    by the formula under test is not evidence. Plot what was measured against what was
    predicted, or plot the residual.
F2. Show the claimed precision. If the claim is agreement to X %, plot the relative error on
    a scale where X % is visible, not two overlaid curves.
F3. Do not collapse dimensions silently. Quantities that depend on (m1, m2) get a 2-D map, or
    are marked by mass ratio. Axes carry units (g_ij in s^-2 in chirp-time coordinates).
F4. Every figure states how it could fail: its caption says what it would look like if the
    result were wrong. A figure for which that sentence cannot be written does not go in
    the report.
F5. No figure from job q12's out/ is reused as an image.
